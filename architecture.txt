Architectures
Arch 1 — Generic AI Baseline
The same assistant as Arch 2 — same model (openai/gpt-oss-120b), same base system prompt, same tools (web search, file sandbox), same voice interface — with the cold-start profile injection disabled. No profile text is added to the system prompt — whether or not a URL has been scraped for that participant — so every participant receives an identical assistant. Represents a competent off-the-shelf AI assistant that happens to know nothing about the user.
Arch 2 — Jarvis with Cold-Start Personalization
Jarvis with URL cold-start active. Before the session, the participant provides one public URL (LinkedIn or GitHub). Jarvis scrapes it, builds a profile summary, and injects it into the system prompt. Represents the personalized architecture being evaluated.
Both architectures run the same model, the same base system prompt, and the same tools. The only difference is whether the URL-derived profile summary is injected into the system prompt — so personalization is the only variable being tested.


Screener Survey
Sent before the session to decide who to invite.
What is your age? (must be 18–25)
What is your major and year in school?
Do you have a public LinkedIn, GitHub, or personal website you would be comfortable sharing for research purposes? (yes / no)
How often do you use AI assistants like ChatGPT or Claude? (never / occasionally / regularly / daily)
What is your personal background — where are you from, what are your interests, and anything else you feel comfortable sharing?

The Three Tasks
Fixed scripts — read to the participant exactly as written. Same 3 tasks for both architectures.
Task 1 — Design a Perfect Outing with Friends
Read to participant:
"Ask the assistant to help you plan a perfect date. It can be a date with someone else or just a fun day out for yourself — whatever feels natural. See how well it matches what you'd actually enjoy."
What we are measuring: Does Arch 2 tailor the date plan to the participant's actual personality, interests, and background from their profile? Does Arch 1 give a generic plan that could apply to anyone?
Success signal for Arch 2: Suggests activities, venues, or experiences that reflect something specific about the participant without them having to explain themselves.

Task 2 — Suggest a Daily Routine
Read to participant:
"Ask the assistant to suggest a daily routine for you — something that fits your lifestyle, goals, and schedule. See whether it actually reflects who you are."
What we are measuring: Does Arch 2 produce a routine that accounts for the participant's field of study, habits, or goals from their profile? Does Arch 1 give a one-size-fits-all routine?
Success signal for Arch 2: Routine reflects the participant's actual background — major, career goals, or lifestyle — without being prompted.

Task 3 — Help Me Find an Internship
Read to participant:
"Ask the assistant to help you find internship opportunities that match your background and interests. See how relevant the suggestions actually are to you."
What we are measuring: Does Arch 2 recommend opportunities tailored to the participant's actual skills, major, and experience? Does Arch 1 give generic advice that doesn't fit?
Success signal for Arch 2: Mentions the participant's specific field, relevant skills, or appropriate experience level without the participant having to explain their background first.

Post-Task Evaluation Survey
Asked after each architecture (same 3 questions repeated for both Arch 1 and Arch 2).
Per task, per architecture:
Did this feel personalized to you? (1–5)
Do the recommendations seem accurate? (yes / partially / no)
Would you trust this suggestion? (1–5)

What to Reset Between Participants
user_profile.json — clear remembered facts and profile summary
preferences.json — clear all preference pairs
user_style.json — clear style summary
